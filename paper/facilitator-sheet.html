Facilitator sheet

The Data Protection Risk Game, paper version. The numbers can be changed. Try them on a table.

Your jobs

Start. Pay each team [10] coins. Turn over the risk cards and tell a short story about them.

Each turn. After the teams have acted, draw an event card and read it out. It affects all teams and does what the card says.

End. Each team rolls its d20 once, in the open. Check every figure on its matrices (below), name the cost and tell what happened.

Does something go wrong?

A figure is safe if the roll is the number of its probability row or above.

12 or above5%, only a natural 1 fails

What does it cost? What happened?

For a figure that fails, look at its impact, the column. The cost is the impact times the impact, in coins. You make up the story.

ImpactCostThe story
1[1] A complaint. Someone writes in, and you answer.
2[4] A report to the authority. Lawyers and forms.
3[9] An inquiry by the authority. Lawyers, and changes you have to make.
4[16] People get hurt. A lawsuit, damages and the newspapers.

A team that cannot pay is out of business.

Optional rule. A team that cannot pay may make a convincing argument to management why it was not their fault. Management, played by you, may cover some or all of the fee.

Optional: the DPO

Optional rule. Only for a high risk: a team with a figure in the red can pay [1] coin to appoint a DPO, who argues why the project must be stopped. Listen, roll your d20 in secret and keep the roll to yourself. Decide in each case whether management buys it. As a guide, a roll of [10] or above means management is open to it, and a good argument can make up for a low roll. If you buy it, the project moves to the next round: the team keeps its matrices and figures, gets the full number of turns again, and has no roll and no check this time. The project is extra to the new risks. The coin is not refunded.

Using your power

You decide on the optional rules and on coins. Use that to reward good decisions, never to hand out coins at random: careless play should cost a team, and careful play should pay.

  • Creative explanation. Give 1 or 2 coins when a team explains well why its measure fits the risk it is working on. Give nothing for a funny story that does not fit.
  • A fee a team cannot pay. Cover part of it when the team's measures fitted its risks. Cover none of it when the measures were scattered.
  • The DPO. Say yes when the argument names a real weakness of the project. The roll is only a guide.
  • Length of the game. More generous with coins and fees makes it longer; stricter makes it shorter. Decide before you start, and keep it the same for every team.

Paper version

Ideas for playing The Data Protection Risk Game at a table, with cards, figures, coins and dice, without the computer. The rulebook itself is rulebook.html, one part at a time. This page keeps the working rules, the decisions, the open questions and the numbers to check.

Working rules

  • The digital game stays the reference for the rules. The paper version is a simpler variant, not a port.
  • A card value is never changed here without asking the designer. If paper play needs a different number, write it down as a proposal.
  • Anything the computer does for the team (arithmetic, shuffling, hidden odds) needs a stand-in on paper.
  • Test each part on a table before the next one is written.

Decided

  • Cards to print: cards.pdf holds the measure, risk and event cards (125 cards, 30 pages, no strategy cards), made with dpia-tabletop pdf. Every second page is the backs. A new sheet starts whenever the back changes (measure, risk, event), and every page of backs has all nine backs, even behind a half-empty sheet of fronts. Card style: the default one (symbol). A second file, cards-text.pdf, has the text-only style (--style style-2), with no pictures.
  • Two or more teams play. The box holds a stack of risk matrices.
  • Optional rules are in and have a blue stripe in the rulebook: the creative explanation, the DPO and the argument to management for a fee that cannot be paid.
  • Player aid and facilitator sheet are their own pages with pictures: player-aid.html and facilitator-sheet.html, each with a DIN A4 PDF.
  • Teams play in parallel. Every team has its own matrix, hand and coins and plays at the same time. There is no turn order and no waiting.
  • The facilitator runs the game: reveals the risks, draws the events, checks the figures after the teams' rolls, tells the story of what went wrong and takes the payments. There are no management cards, and no recommendation or management decision step.
  • A project is the unit. No quarters or years. Each project starts with coins paid to every team and ends with the roll and the consequences. The score is the number of projects lasted.
  • Swapping cards: a project has four turns, and a team has one action in each. Instead of playing a card, a team may swap up to [3] cards from its hand for new ones from the draw pile. It costs [1] coin and uses up the turn. There is no buying of cards.
  • Turns: a project has several turns. In each turn every team plays a card, and then the facilitator draws one event card that affects all teams. After the last turn each team rolls and the facilitator checks the figures.
  • Cards: one draw pile and one discard pile for the whole table; every team holds its own hand. Costs are paid when a card is played; cards are swapped, not bought. A played project card goes to the discard pile, a shareable (blue) card is piled face down in front of the team, which picks the pile up into its hand at the end of the project, a central (yellow) card stays on the table. In a turn in which a team plays anything but a blue card, it draws a new one when it plays it. Strategy cards are not part of the paper version. When the pile is empty the discard pile is reshuffled.
  • Matrices and figures: setup does not hand out a matrix. The facilitator lays the matrices and figures ready, and every team takes one matrix per risk at the start of each project.
  • Events: an event does what its card says. It affects all teams.
  • Risk board: one risk matrix per risk (a 4×4 grid of spaces joined by lines, zones in colour) with coloured figures that are moved. Every team gets at least one matrix, and a team that fights two risks at once uses two. Mockup: risk-matrix.png.
  • Trouble: at the end of the project each team rolls its own d20 once, in the open. The facilitator checks every figure on the team's matrices: the probability row says which roll the team needs to be safe (2, 8, 14 or 20 or above), so a natural 1 always fails and a 20 always saves. For a figure that fails, the cost is the impact times the impact in coins (1, 4, 9, 16), and the facilitator makes up the story of what happened. The only die is the d20, one per team.
  • A game is a fixed number of projects. Teams still in business at the end are ranked by coins left. There is always a facilitator who does not play; there is no solo mode in the core rules.
  • [DPO (optional): at the end of a project a team may pay [1] coin and appoint a data protection officer who argues why the project must be stopped. The facilitator rolls a d20 in secret and decides in each case whether management buys it. If so, the project moves to the next round: the team keeps its matrices and figures, gets another chance for mitigation, and has no roll and no check this time. The full number of turns applies, the project is extra to the new risks (revealed for all teams as usual), the next round pays coins as usual, and the DPO coin is not refunded.]
  • [Creative explanation (optional): when a team plays a card, the processing owner explains, as creatively as they can, why the measure works perfectly here, and the facilitator judges it. Possible effects (not decided): a coin back, one extra step or card, a re-roll at the end of the project, or just a laugh.]
  • Coins: the facilitator gives every team its starting coins at setup and can add or remove some to balance the teams. At the start of every project, every team is paid [4] more coins.
  • Scoring is per team, so the room can compare how long each stayed in business.

Open questions

  • Numbers to try: starting coins [12], coins at the start of a project [4], hand size [7], swap up to [3] cards for [1] coin, number of projects [6], number of risks per project. The numbers are fine for the first playtest. See the table below.
  • Creative explanation (optional rule, in): what a good explanation earns (a coin back, one extra step or card, a re-roll at the end of the project, or just a laugh).
  • DPO (optional rule, in): whether to keep it after the playtest. It brings the facilitator's secret roll and a project that is carried over to the next round.
  • Events that switch a measure off: they need a short rule on paper.
  • Print layout: is the printed deck (dpia-tabletop pdf) enough, or do the paper cards need their own layout?
  • Playtest: the rulebook has not been played on a table yet.

Wording on the cards (for the designer)

The paper rules use words that also work on the cards. No card text has been changed. These cards would need a change to match:

  • Card slot becomes action (11 events): EV11 and EV21 to EV30 say "card slot" ("lose one card slot for this project"). On paper one action is one turn, so "lose one action in this project".
  • REUSE and GLOBAL (36 measures): "you can REUSE it in every project now for 2 extra cost per project" would read "comes back to your hand after you play it, playing it again costs 2"; the yellow text can stay.
  • Quarter and year (3 events): EV05, EV12 and EV32 name a quarter or a year. Only the budget freeze (it ends when the quarter ends) is a rule; it would end with the project.
  • Strategy cards: not in the paper version, so they stay out of the paper deck. Events that switch a measure off still need a short rule on paper.

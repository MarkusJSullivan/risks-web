Set up the table: the middle

Rulebook 3. Setup
page 1
  1. Shuffle all measure cards (grey, blue and yellow together) and put them in the middle: the draw pile. Leave room next to it for the discard pile.
  2. Shuffle the risk cards into one stack and the event cards into another. Put both next to the draw pile.
  3. Put a stack of matrices and the figures (one colour for each goal) where every team can reach them.
  4. Keep the facilitator sheet, a d20 and the coins at hand.

For today’s pretend play stack the draw pile so the first six cards are, from the top: 1. Answer, don't keep, 2. Only your own patch, 3. Somebody reads the log, 4. Separate drawers, 5. Who covers on Friday?, 6. Names stay in the vault.

“Before we start, we set up the table together. Follow the numbers on the picture.” Share your screen and do it with your own cards if you have them.

Set up the table: each team

Rulebook 3. Setup
page 2
  1. Give each team a player aid and a d20.
  2. Give each team its starting coins: 20.
  3. Deal each team 9 cards from the draw pile.
  4. Each team picks a processing owner: they hold the cards and the coins.

For today’s pretend play the two hands are fixed: you see each hand on the page of its first turn. Risk cards in order: Real data in the test system, then Staff snoop in records. Event cards in order: A coupon, with a smile, Turns out it was a lot worse, Mandatory fun, Now with oat milk. Dice: Otter’s roll is 12, your secret roll for Heron’s DPO is 13.

“Now each team gets its things.” Two teams today: Otter and Heron.

Project 1: the start

Rulebook 4.1 Start of a project
page 3
  • Both teams get 10 coins: 30 each.
  • The risk card lists, for each goal, two numbers: the probability (how likely) is the row, and the impact (how bad) is the column. Up and right is worse.
  • M (orange): probability 3, impact 2 → the figure goes to row 3, column 2.
  • C (black): probability 3, impact 4 → the figure goes to row 3, column 4.
  • The red spaces (probability × impact above 8) are the ones to get out of. C is in the red now.
  • No yellow card is on a table yet, so there are no yellow moves.
Otter: 30 coins
Heron: 30 coins

“New project. I pay each of you 10 coins.” Turn over risk card R03 and tell the story: “Someone copied the live customer data into the test system, just to try something. Now twelve testers and a contractor can see everything.”

End of project 1 · Before the dice: Heron calls in the DPO

Rulebook 4.3 optional rule: call in the DPO, before the roll (only for a high risk)
page 11
  • Heron pays 1 coin (not refunded): 26 → 25.
  • The project moves to the next round: Heron keeps its matrix and figures, and gets a full set of turns again.
  • Heron has no roll and no check this time: no bill for this round.
  • The round still ends: Heron turns its yellow card upright again (blue cards too). An upright yellow card at the start of the next round means: its moves are still to make.
  • R03 is now a moved project: blue and yellow cards are not played on it any more. Only grey cards move its figures.
Heron: 26 → 25 coins

“The project is over. Before anybody rolls: does a team want to call in the DPO?” Heron's M stands at probability 3, impact 3 (row 3, column 3): that is a red space, a high risk. Heron may call in the DPO for 1 coin.

“Heron, give me one reason management should stop this project.” Let Heron name a real weakness.

Roll your own d20 in secret. Script: 13. Ten or above: management is open to it. Say: “Management buys it.”

What if…

Side steps for the same moments
page 16
Otter cannot pay. The bill is 9 coins, but Otter has only 6. A team that cannot pay is out of business. Optional rule: Otter argues it was not its fault; if you are convinced you cover some of the fee, for example half (5 coins): Otter pays 4, stays in and has 2 coins left. Cover none of it if the measures were scattered.
Heron’s DPO is refused. With a secret roll below 10 and a weak argument, management does not buy it. Heron has paid the coin and goes on with its roll like any team.
A team cannot pay for a card. It picks a cheaper card or swaps.
A figure would leave the matrix. It stops at 1 or at 4. A plus on a figure at 4 does nothing.

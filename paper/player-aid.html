Player aid

Start of a project

Hand of [9] cards. Take a matrix for every risk. On the risk card, left number = probability, right number = impact: put the figure of that colour on that space. Then make the moves of every upright yellow card, and turn it sideways.

Each turn: one action

Play a card. The processing owner plays a measure card, pays its coins and moves the figures on the matrix.

Optional rule. Explain, as creatively as you can, why this measure works perfectly here. A good reason can earn 1 or 2 coins from the facilitator.

Swap cards. Put up to [5] cards on the discard pile and draw as many. Not blue cards lying sideways. It is free. This is your action.

Then the facilitator draws an event card. It affects all teams.

Card colours

Grey, project. Played once, then onto the discard pile. Draw a new card.

Blue, shareable. Lay it face up and turned sideways next to your team. Once per project. It is still part of your hand. At the next project turn it upright: you may play it again at its replay price. No new card.

Yellow, central. Face up on the table. Make its moves now and turn it sideways. At the end of the project turn it upright: upright at the start means its moves are still to make. Draw a new card.

End of a project

Roll your d20 once (after the DPO call, if you make one). The facilitator checks every figure on your matrices. Roll too low for the probability row of a figure and you pay for its impact: [1], [4], [9] or [16] coins for impact 1 to 4.

Coins. [20] to start, then [10] at the start of every project. A team that cannot pay is out of business.

The matrix

Optional rule. Cannot pay? Argue to management why it was not your fault. It may cover some or all of the fee.
Optional rule, before the roll. A figure in the red? Pay [1] coin and call in a DPO to argue why the project must be stopped. The facilitator decides. If bought: no roll and no cost, and the risk comes back next round with the same figures. Only grey cards move the figures of that old matrix, not blue or yellow ones.

Reading a card

Do it on every matrix that has a figure of that colour. A figure never leaves the matrix: it stays between 1 and 4.
